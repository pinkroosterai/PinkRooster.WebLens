Rare Double Rainbow Draws Crowds to Saltmere Waterfront

A vivid double rainbow arched over Saltmere's harbor for nearly twenty minutes on Tuesday evening, drawing residents out of shops and cafes along the waterfront promenade to watch. Traffic along Quay Street slowed noticeably as drivers pulled over for a better view.

The phenomenon appeared shortly after a brief afternoon shower cleared, as low sun broke through parting clouds to the west. Local meteorologist Dennis Okafor said the combination of a low sun angle and large raindrops left over from the shower produced unusually vivid, saturated bands of colour.

"You need three things for a bright double rainbow: sunlight from a fairly low angle, largish raindrops, and a dark cloud behind them to set off the colours," Okafor said. "Tuesday had all three, which is why so many people were out taking photos." He added that the display lasted longer than most, likely because the rain cell moved offshore slowly.

Photos of the rainbow, framed against the harbor's crane silhouettes, quickly circulated on local social media groups, with several residents comparing it to a similar display seen over Saltmere a decade ago.

Double rainbows form when light reflects twice inside a raindrop rather than once, producing a fainter secondary arc outside the primary one, with its colours reversed. The secondary bow is usually harder to see, which made Tuesday's clear appearance notable.

Cafes along the promenade reported a brief rush as the display drew a crowd that lingered well past the evening rush hour, with several vendors saying it was the busiest the waterfront had been on a weekday evening all summer. One barista said she lost count of how many customers asked to step outside mid-order just to look.
