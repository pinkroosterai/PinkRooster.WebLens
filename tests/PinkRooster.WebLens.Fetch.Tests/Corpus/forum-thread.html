Trailhead 38 after 200 miles — worth it?

Posted in Gear Reviews · 14 replies · 2.1k views

ridgeline_rae
2 Jul 2026, 2:05pm

I've put about two hundred miles on my Trailhead 38 since May, mostly three-day loops in the foothills. The hip belt has held up better than I expected for a pack in this weight class, and the side pockets are deep enough that my bottle has never popped out on a scramble. I've also used it as a carry-on a couple of times when driving to trailheads that require an early flight, and it compresses down enough to fit the sizer at most gate counters.

My one complaint is the top lid zipper, which has started to catch a bit. Not a dealbreaker yet, but I'm keeping an eye on it before a longer trip in the fall.

— Rae, Cascade foothills
switchback_sam
2 Jul 2026, 6:47pm

the top lid zipper, which has started to catch a bit

Same zipper issue here after about 150 miles. I sprayed a bit of dry silicone lubricant on the track and it's been smooth since. Worth trying before you write it off.

Also make sure you're not overtightening the lid — I found leaving a millimeter of play in the strap keeps tension off the zipper pull itself, which seems to be where mine started rubbing.

— Sam

ridgeline_rae
3 Jul 2026, 8:30pm

It carries fine past 30 pounds as long as you're using the compression straps — without them the load shifts more than I'd like on descents. And yes, the frame sheet comes out through a sleeve at the back panel, takes about a minute.

For what it's worth, I also swapped the stock hip belt pad for the women's-specific version they sell separately, and it fits my hips noticeably better on long days.

— Rae
gearhead_greg
5 Jul 2026, 11:58am

Long-time lurker, first post. I run a Trailhead 38 as my go-to for anything under five days and have replaced exactly one part: the sternum strap buckle cracked after a fall on granite last winter. Manufacturer sent a replacement free of charge.

Otherwise the fabric has shrugged off two seasons of bushwhacking without a single tear. Solid pack for the price.

If you're deciding between the 38 and the smaller 28-liter version, I'd say go up a size if you ever plan on winter trips with a bulkier sleeping bag.

— Greg
